of a 7,043-customer base was leaving. Nobody could see why — the answers were split across five disconnected tables.

IBM Telco — Customer Churn

I modeled the five tables into a SQL Server star schema, then wrote 15 T-SQL queries structured as a story: how big, who, where, why, and what's at risk.

A Python pipeline handled the cleaning — domain-aware imputation, IQR capping with a zero-variance guard, and removal of every target-leakage column before modeling.

The finding that mattered: month-to-month customers churn 18× more than two-year ones — 45.8% against 2.5%. Four connected Tableau dashboards turned that into five retention recommendations, each tied to a number.

in sales sat behind a data model that was quietly broken — relationships wired to the wrong keys, and a fiscal calendar nobody had verified.

Wide World Importers — Sales Analytics

I started from a raw model with broken relationships and no visuals. Rebuilding the star schema came first: one fact table, five dimensions, every key corrected.

Then Power Query cleaning — including a custom column that derives product colour from its name against an 871-colour lookup — and a 24+ measure DAX library split across five measure tables.

I also verified the company's real fiscal year start from the data itself (November, not the calendar default) before writing a single time-intelligence formula. The report ends on strategy: California drives 44.4% of sales, and the top 20% of customers produce 47.9% of all profit.
